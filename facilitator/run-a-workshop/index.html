Run a workshop end to end

This is the facilitator’s main path: create a board, open the room, move the group through the phases, and finish with an export. The design stance to keep in mind is that phases are your agenda broadcast, not a permission system. Almost nothing is blocked; a phase change retunes defaults and hints for the whole room.

Your home screen

Section titled “Your home screen”

Facilitating starts from an account: register with a username, email, and password, or sign in (a forgotten password can be reset from the sign-in screen). Home has two tabs:

  • New: create a workshop (named after the engagement, with an optional customer) and its first board.
  • Existing: a searchable list of your workshops and boards with lifecycle badges, where you copy join links and reopen past boards. You can also import a board file here, which recreates a workshop from the file with its full history - the way to resume an engagement exported elsewhere.

Before the room arrives

Section titled “Before the room arrives”
  1. Sign in, then New workshop. Name it after the engagement and set the customer. The board name mirrors the workshop name; override it when one workshop will storm several use cases (one board per use case).
  2. Open the board. This matters: joins are rejected until you enter (“the board is not open yet”). Your arrival opens the room.
  3. Invite. Copy a link: a participant link or a read-only observer link - the link a person opens sets their role, and it is enforced server-side. Both are on the home screen after you create the board and in the in-board invite dialog. People join as guests (name only) or by signing in; duplicate names are auto-suffixed.
  4. Decide the sticky-creation mode (Settings, Board tab): open for small trusted rooms, moderated for large or unruly ones. See Submission tray and moderation.
  5. Optional: set the inactivity timeout (default 30 minutes). You are exempt.

Moving between phases

Section titled “Moving between phases”

The phase control is a transport: previous, a dropdown, and next, plus a minutes box for the shared timer (which has its own play, pause, stop, restart, and extend controls - see Timers and timeboxes). Phases never auto-advance, and the dropdown locks while a clock runs. Every switch shows a one-shot briefing card to everyone (role-aware): what to do now, what changed in the tool, and a suggested view you can switch to with one click. Views stay local to each person; use presenting when you need the whole room in one view.

Work the phases in order (the Per-phase playbook has the detail for each):

  1. Concept alignment: agree vocabulary and place the cast.
  2. Use-case framing: pick the journeys worth storming.
  3. Storming: dump events, order the timeline, wire the chains, circle contexts.
  4. Event architecture: enrich the pivotal and most-voted events.
  5. Build planning: assign plan tags, and close every hotspot - record the room’s decision on it, or give it an owner to carry forward.

Testing the story

Section titled “Testing the story”

Before you call the storm done, make the wall prove itself: select an event and ⟲ walk the story (side panel). At every hop the tool asks both questions along the accepted links - ”← what made this possible?” and ”→ what does this set in motion?” - lighting the chain and its wires while the rest of the wall recedes, the camera following each step. Walked backwards, a sound story completes at an actor (”✓ the story begins here - Rider sets it in motion”); walked forwards it ends at an outcome. Anywhere it breaks, the break is phrased as the question to put to the room - “nothing produces this fact - what command causes it?” - and the answer is either a missing link to draw or a hotspot to park. The walk moves only your camera; present first and the whole room walks with you. Drag the walk card by its header if it sits on something you need to see.

Tidying the wall

Section titled “Tidying the wall”

When the storm settles, auto-layout (✨, top of the left rail; active on the plain view - lane views tidy themselves) re-renders the whole wall in one pass: members are packed into their approved boundaries, everything unassigned lands in a row below, and hotspots gather at the bottom. It asks for confirmation first, because it moves real stickies (the moves are logged like any other) and is not a single-step undo. The ◷ origin lens still remembers the original chaos, so nothing about the storm’s history is lost - and it makes a good “look how far we came” moment right after the pass.

Closing the session

Section titled “Closing the session”
  1. Close the hotspots: select each one and record the room’s decision in the side panel’s Resolution section (see Hotspots and decisions) - the sticky settles (muted, ✓) but stays on the wall, and the decision lands in the workbook’s Hotspots & Decisions sheet. Questions the room could not answer get an owner instead. Board Review counts anything that got neither. (This works before or after finalizing - a resolution is decision-capture, like plan tags, not storming.)
  2. Finalize (Settings, Board, lifecycle): storming locks for everyone; enrichment, plan tags, hotspot resolutions, and exports stay open. Finalized means “the storm is closed”, not “read-only” - and it is reversible: reopen the board from the same place if the room needs another pass.
  3. Export (top right): the workbook for the account team, the board file (the full event log, the only artifact you truly need), the SVG snapshot, and the Event Portal import file. See Export formats.
  4. Replay as the closing act: drag the slider from zero and the workshop rebuilds itself, captioned with who did what. See Replay and history.
  5. Ending options, in escalating strength: just leave (the room keeps working), end the session for everyone, or archive (permanent and read-only, with exports and replay still working).
