The story walk

Event Storming’s classic consistency check is telling the story backwards: start from the outcome and ask “what made this possible?” until the chain completes - or breaks, and the break is the finding. The ⟲ story walk is that check as a tool feature, extended to run in both directions.

Starting a walk

Section titled “Starting a walk”

Select any event and press ⟲ walk the story in its side panel. The walk card appears, the chain you have walked stays lit (stickies and the wires between them glow as if selected), the rest of the wall recedes, and the camera glides to each node as you step. Drag the card by its header if it sits on something you need to see; Esc ends the walk and everything returns to normal.

The two questions

Section titled “The two questions”

At every node the card asks both:

  • ← what made this possible? The node’s causes, along the accepted links: an event is made possible by its command, a command by the policy that issues it, a policy by the event that triggers it.
  • → what does this set in motion? The node’s consequences: an event triggers its policies (and feeds its read models), a policy issues its commands, a command produces its events.

Each candidate is a button; clicking it steps the walk. There is deliberately no auto-advance, even when there is only one candidate - ask the room the question first, then let the wall answer. A ↻ on a candidate means the walk has already visited it: a causal loop, worth a look in itself.

How walks end

Section titled “How walks end”
  • ✓ The story begins here. Walking backwards reaches a command with a named actor - a human sets the chain in motion. This is the success state: the story holds, end to end.
  • The chain breaks. Every break is phrased as the question to put to the room: “nothing produces this fact - what command causes it?”, “nobody issues this command - which actor decides, or which policy reacts?”, “nothing triggers this policy - which event wakes it?”. Walking forwards, the endings mirror the soft hints: an event nothing reacts to (an outcome - or a dead end worth a hotspot), a policy that issues no command, a command that produces no event. A read model ends a branch gracefully: consulted, not causal.
  • Either way, the remedy is the same pair: draw the missing link, or park a hotspot - then walk again.

Walking the whole room

Section titled “Walking the whole room”

The walk moves only your camera and selection - like every lens, it is local. But that is exactly what presenting mirrors: present first, then walk, and every hop - camera, lit chain, selected sticky - plays on every screen in the room. This is the strongest storm-closing move in the facilitator’s kit: put the outcome event on stage and let the wall prove its story in front of everyone.

When to use it

Section titled “When to use it”
  • Closing the storm: the playbook’s exit cue - move on when the walk from the outcome event survives to an actor.
  • Opening event architecture: a quick walk re-tells the story before the room switches from discovery to design.
  • Any time a chain looks suspicious: a two-hop walk is faster than reading wires.

The walk reads the live wall through its links, so it steps aside during playback (which shows the historical wall) and in the ◷ origin lens (which shows birth positions, where links never render).
