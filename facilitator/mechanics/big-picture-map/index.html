Running the Big Picture map

The map is where a workshop starts and where it keeps coming back. Its job is not to model anything precisely - it is to let the room agree what exists, who owns it, and what to storm next. If you have not read How a workshop is shaped, start there.

Before the room arrives

Section titled “Before the room arrives”

Creating the workshop already made the map, and you landed on it. Everything you would normally set up on a board applies here: open the room (joins are refused until you do), send the two role-scoped invite links, and decide whether sticky creation is open or moderated.

The palette is trimmed on a map: events, actors, systems, hotspots, and the two map-only cards. Commands, policies, aggregates and read models are absent on purpose - they belong to a flow board, and offering them here invites the room to start modelling one story before it has agreed on the landscape.

The left rail ships collapsed. Open it when you need the palette; the wall is the point.

The first pass: collect, do not organize

Section titled “The first pass: collect, do not organize”

Ask for things that happen in the business, in past tense, from anyone. Take them fast and let them land anywhere - the map’s plain wall is deliberately a flood, not a timeline. Park every argument as a hotspot the moment it loops a second time.

Two facilitator moves make this pass worth the time:

  • Say “coarse”. “Order placed” belongs here; “Order line quantity validated” does not. If an event only makes sense inside one team’s process, it is a flow-board event.
  • Star the ones that cross the business (★). On a map, pivotal marks an event more than one part of the business cares about - which is exactly the Shared flag in Event Portal later.

Naming domains

Section titled “Naming domains”

A Domain card is a candidate slice: first line is its name, the rest is a one-line pitch. “Reservations - booking a room, from search to confirmed stay.” Write the pitch. It is what stops “Reservations” meaning three different things to three people, it decides the scope of the board you are about to create, and it becomes the domain’s description in Event Portal.

This is the one thing the room dot-votes on the map. Voting here is about sequence, not merit - which slice earns the next hour.

Naming teams

Section titled “Naming teams”

A Team card names an org unit that can own things. Committing the text adds it to the workshop’s Teams table, which then feeds the owner suggestions on every board and the owning-team field in the export. That is the whole point: the room spells “Payments squad” once, here, instead of four ways across four boards.

Seating: teams and domains views

Section titled “Seating: teams and domains views”

Switch the view to teams or domains. Each card anchors a lane; drag events and systems into the lane of whoever is expected to own them, and back to the dock to un-seat. The dock is the useful half of the picture - it shows what the room has not placed.

A seat is a prediction. A ★ on a flow board is a claim. When they disagree later, the review says “seated in one domain, claimed by another” and you have a real conversation to run rather than a silent inconsistency.

Approving a domain

Section titled “Approving a domain”

Select the card, then ✔ approve domain in the side panel. A board appears in the rail, empty, named from the card’s first line, and everyone in the room sees the announcement without refreshing.

Approving twice is harmless - you get the same board back, not a second one. An unnamed card is refused, because a board named after a blank card helps nobody.

Revert un-approves. It deletes the child board only while the board is untouched; after that the tool refuses and tells you to delete it at the board level. Deleting a stormed wall is a decision, never a side effect.

Checking the map is done enough

Section titled “Checking the map is done enough”

The ✓ review on a map runs its own six checks - no domain candidates yet, a domain with no pitch, open questions with no owner, flow-board stickies that wandered onto the map, no teams named, events with no home domain - and, once you have more than one board, the workshop tier on top.

There is no ✦ AI review on a map. The questions here have exact answers.

What the map does not have

Section titled “What the map does not have”

No phases and no timeboxes: collecting the landscape is not a five-act structure, so the phase stepper is absent and the ? card explains the map instead. No board topic sub-root (the workshop root is set here, since it belongs to the whole workshop), no lane attributes in the side panel, no flow links (seat things instead of wiring them), and no per-board Event Portal export

  • the map exports the merged workshop file, not a domain of its own.
