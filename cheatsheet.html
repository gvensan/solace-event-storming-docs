Event Storming, before you walk in

participant cheatsheet · 5-minute read

You are joining a workshop where the room reconstructs how the business actually works by putting facts that happened on a shared timeline. No slides, no spec documents. The knowledge in your head is the raw material, which means you cannot prepare, and you cannot be underqualified.

The one rule worth remembering

Events are written in the past tense, because they happened.

Order placed Payment declined Guest checked in If you can say "...and then X happened", X belongs on the wall.

1You will write these

Domain event "It happened." Booking confirmed · Guest checked in
Hotspot "We disagree, or we don't know." Who owns overbooking?
Actor "Who did it." Guest · Front-desk agent
External system "The world outside." Payment gateway · Email service

2These emerge as the story firms up - recognize them, don't memorize them

Command "Someone wants it done." Check in guest → Guest checked in
Policy "Whenever X, then Y." On no-show, charge the fee
Aggregate "The thing it happens to." Reservation · Room
Read model "What someone checks before deciding." Tonight's arrivals list, read by the front desk

Two yellows are deliberate: bright lemon for actors (people), deeper honey for aggregates (things). The printed label on every sticky settles any doubt.

On the workshop's map board, two extra cards appear and the deeper kinds disappear: a chalk Domain card (a slice of the business worth its own board - first line names it, the rest is the pitch) and a slate Team card (who can own things). A map collects the coarse picture; the story of any one slice gets stormed on its own board.

3How they connect - the loop the wall will grow

You never build this diagram by hand - it grows out of the stickies as the room wires them up. Recognizing the shape is enough.

5Rules of the room

Duplicates are fine. Wrong is fine. Silence is the only failure. Someone already wrote your sticky? Good - it means it matters.

Arguing about what a word means is not a distraction. The argument is the deliverable.

Disagreements don't get settled mid-flow. Park them as red hotspots - often the most valuable output of the day.

No solutions yet. "Which database" and "which topic name" have their own phase later. Park it.

You don't know the whole flow? Nobody does. That is exactly why everyone is in the room.

6The arc of the session

1 · ConceptAgree what words mean

name the actors, the systems, the vocabulary

2 · Use casePick the story

choose the journey worth storming

3 · StormingThe storm

dump events, order the timeline, wire the flow

4 · ArchitectureDesign how the system tells it

events become real, addressable messages

5 · Build planWho builds what

owners and order; every hotspot gets a name

→ after the roomThe model goes to work

your events land in the organization's event catalog (Solace Event Portal)

Phases 1 to 3 are discovery - your part is writing and arguing. Phases 4 and 5 shift to design: the facilitator drives, and your part shifts to advising and objecting early. The dashed box is not part of the session - it is where the work lands, and the reason precision today pays.

7In the tool

Join via the invite link - your name, no account. The link sets what you can do.

Add a sticky: pick a color, double-click the wall, type, Enter.

Move by dragging. Views are yours alone - switching moves nothing for anyone else.

Vote 👍 on any sticky once the storm opens - it steers what gets attention first.

Chat 💬 any time; the little ? always answers "what can I do right now?".

The wall does not die in a photo. Everything you write becomes a living model the architects carry forward after the session - which is why a sticky in your words, even a wrong one, is worth more than a polished one that never got written.
